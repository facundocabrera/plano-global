## Coordinate model

`Phase` is reserved for the structural multi-year regime. `Stage` describes the tactical sequence observed over weeks or months.

| Tactical stage | Description | Transition signal |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Stress and repricing | Stress reaches sovereigns, credit or funding. |
| Stage 2A | Early fragility | Long-end pressure persists and spreads. |
| Stage 2B | Advanced fragility / rates-led divergence | Bond dysfunction, credit break or repeated intervention. |
| Stage 3 | Intervention | Scale, persistence and an administered long financing cost. |
| Stage 4 | Expansion after stabilization | Intervention becomes normalized or reignites inflation/excess. |

Tactical Stage 3 does not by itself confirm structural Phase 2A. A one-off operation may be interventionist without removing long-rate price discovery persistently.

## Monthly dashboard

- Energy: Brent, WTI, TTF, Henry Hub, routes and refining capacity.
- Sovereign curve: UST 2Y/10Y/30Y, 2s10s, 5s30s, gilts and Bunds.
- Fiscal risk: term premium, auction tails, bid-to-cover, dealer take-down and foreign official demand.
- Volatility/funding: MOVE, VIX, Treasury liquidity, SOFR/repo and FRA-OIS.
- Credit: HY OAS, IG OAS, defaults, private credit and bank lending standards.
- Japan/carry: USDJPY, JPY crosses, JGB 10Y/20Y/30Y and BOJ policy.
- Inflation/real yields: 5Y/10Y breakevens, 10Y TIPS and expectation surveys.
- Dollar/reserves: DXY and FX-adjusted COFER shares.
- Gold: price versus DXY and real yields, official tonnes and price-adjusted reserve share.
- Real economy: PMIs, trade, Baltic Dry, employment and participation.
- Real-asset rotation: energy, metals, infrastructure, agriculture and strategic producers.

## Master signals

1. Yield suppression: long yields stop rising while breakevens remain elevated, preferably with an observable institutional mechanism.
2. Gold decoupling: gold rises despite a firm DXY and/or high real yields, reinforced by official physical purchases.
3. Credit/funding stress: HY OAS, MOVE and funding measures rise together.
4. Japan carry repricing: sudden yen strength coincides with risk-asset weakness.
5. Long-end fiscal stress: the long end rises while the front end is contained and auction quality deteriorates.
6. Energy coordination breakdown: oil remains elevated despite weak growth because of routes, refining or producer coordination.
